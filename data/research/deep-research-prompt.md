# DEEP RESEARCH TASK: Where worldschooling / traveling families actually CLUSTER

> Portable prompt — feed to any LLM with web access to reproduce / extend the research in
> `family-gathering-places-2026-06-08.md`. It bakes in the distinctions learned the hard
> way (families ≠ nomads, family-season ≠ tourist-season, organic nesting vs. program
> clustering, the Israeli/Hebrew sub-type, and "a homepage read is not a source read").
> Copy everything below the line.

---

You are a meticulous research analyst. Produce a ranked, evidence-based catalogue of
**places where traveling, "nesting," or worldschooling families gather, base, or settle
for a season** — and where Israeli families in particular cluster. Research in **English
AND Hebrew** (the Hebrew/Israeli scene is large and under-represented in English sources).

## THE SIGNAL I WANT (and the four things people get wrong)

1. **Families ≠ digital nomads.** These are two different communities. They overlap only
   on "working online" and usually do NOT mix; their destinations coincide only sometimes
   (often just fast internet). COUNT family-sourced evidence only. Park pure digital-nomad
   sources (Nomads.com, nomad-fest listicles, "best nomad cities") in a SEPARATE section
   and exclude them from counts. A place can be both (Bali, Lisbon, Chiang Mai, Bansko) —
   keep it in the family table ONLY if it has independent *family* references; tag it `F+N`.

2. **Family-hub season ≠ tourist high season.** The window families actually cluster is
   often different from — sometimes the opposite of — the tourist peak. For every place,
   report the *family-clustering* months, not the tourist season, and flag it for
   validation. Known traps to check for explicitly:
   - **Northern Thailand burning season (~Feb–Apr, haze can spill Dec→Jun)** empties Pai &
     Chiang Mai — families leave; their window is the cool, clear **Nov–early Feb**.
   - **Monsoon blackouts:** Goa (Jun–Sep), Koh Lanta (May–Oct), SE Asia broadly; Hoi An
     floods Oct–Nov; Yucatán/Caribbean hurricanes Jun–Oct.
   - **Northern-hemisphere rhythm:** families cluster around summer (Jun–Aug) and the
     Dec–Jan holidays; European/Israeli summer hubs (Bansko, Romania, Greece, Italy) peak
     then, while tropical hubs peak in the opposite (dry-winter) window.
   - **Same town can host two different things in two seasons** (e.g. a paid *winter*
     worldschool pop-up program vs. an organic *summer* family community). If sources
     disagree on season, SPLIT into two records rather than averaging.

3. **Organic nesting vs. program-driven clustering are different — label which.** Some
   places are organic family communities (Chiang Mai, Bali). Others cluster only because
   an organizer runs fixed-date programs there (Deliberate Detour 2-week immersions;
   Project World School teen/family retreats; Boundless Life cohorts). Both count, but tag
   the mechanism so they're not conflated.

4. **Reference count = # of DISTINCT family sources you actually READ that name the place
   as somewhere families gather/nest.** It's a rough *consensus* score, NOT popularity or
   quality, and it's biased by which posts you fetched — say so. Facebook/WhatsApp groups
   are NOT counted (login-walled), but note where real clustering is much bigger than the
   number shows (especially the Israeli communities).

5. **Hubs are not nationality-neutral — map the national skew of each.** Different hubs draw
   different nationalities, and that decides who a family finds there and **what language the
   kids play in.** Examples: **Pai = very Israeli** (but not only); **Koh Lanta = very Swedish**
   (the "Swedish island," ~70–80% Swedish, two Swedish schools — but not only); **Goa** splits
   Israeli (Arambol) vs. Russian (Morjim); **Bali** skews Australian; **Penang** has a deep
   German society; **Bansko** skews Dutch/British/Israeli. The core nationalities to look for:
   **Israel, USA, Holland, Germany, Sweden, England** — plus Russia, Australia, Canada,
   Scandinavia-broadly (add others you find). Two traps: (a) **visibility ≠ share** — a
   community can *feel* dominant (signage, Chabad, a school) while being a minority of headcount
   (Pai: Israelis are highly visible but the UK is the #1 visitor nationality); (b)
   **tourist-nationality ≠ nesting-family-nationality.** The richest evidence lives in
   login-walled *national* FB groups ("Svenskar på Koh Lanta," etc.) — flag those as the next
   manual pass and give your table a confidence level per row.

## METHOD (do this, don't shortcut)

- **Do not judge a blog by its homepage.** Homepages are almost always thin (generic
  vacation/itinerary content). Go to the specific posts: "worldschooling hubs," "where we
  based," "X months in [place]," extended-stay / slow-travel / relocation posts, and
  hub-roundup listicles. A homepage read is NOT a source read.
- Mine BOTH: (a) structured worldschooling-hub directories, and (b) individual family
  journals/blogs in English and Hebrew. Hebrew press (Ynet, Maariv, Mako) and Israeli
  family blogs are first-class sources for the Israeli sub-type.
- For each place, capture: independent family sources, the family-clustering season, and
  one line of *why* families cluster there.
- **Everything is UNVALIDATED.** It's blog/press-derived. Mark it so; never present a
  count or season as confirmed.

## DISTINCT SUB-TYPE TO BREAK OUT: Israeli intentional / nesting communities

Surface these separately — they're pure nesting-family communities (not nomads) and often
live in login-walled Hebrew Facebook groups, so they're under-counted. Examples that exist
and are worth using as seeds/sanity checks: Israeli forest/green schools in Koh Phangan;
Israeli communal villages in Greece, Italy (kibbutz-style), Portugal (eco-villages),
Bulgaria; Israeli relocation bases like Cyprus (Limassol), Cascais/Lisbon, Goa (Arambol),
Playa del Carmen, Pai, Bansko. Treat these as a candidate distinct category/map tag.

## OUTPUT FORMAT

**Section A — Family gathering / nesting places (ranked by reference count).** A table:
`# | Place | Country | Community (F = families · IF = notable Israeli-family presence ·
F+N = family clustering that also overlaps a nomad scene) | Season (family-hub) | Why
families cluster | Refs (R-keys) | Count`. Add a "⚠️ validate" flag on any season you're
not confident about, with a short note explaining the doubt.

**Section A-notes — Seasonality & validation traps:** call out specific discrepancies and
the traps above, per place.

**Section A-nat — Nationality skew per hub:** a table `Place | Dominant / notable family
nationalities | Evidence | Confidence`. Note the visibility-vs-share and tourist-vs-family
caveats, and flag national FB groups as the next manual pass.

**Section B — Israeli intentional / nesting communities:** their own table (Community |
Place | Country | Note | Ref).

**Section C — Digital-nomad hubs (NOT family clustering):** listed only so they don't
pollute Section A; explicitly excluded from counts.

**Section D — Weak signal: itinerary / curriculum / sightseeing destinations:** single-
mention "places to visit," not clustering (Rome, Paris, theme parks, etc.).

**Reference key:** number every source R1, R2, … Tag each `[EN]`/`[HE]` and mark `[✓]` if
you actually read & extracted from it vs. `[lead]` if it's only catalogued for a later
pass. For each `[✓]`, give a one-line finding. Aim for 100+ sources; be honest about how
many you truly read vs. listed.

## ALSO DISTINGUISH (don't blur these three)

- **Family gathering/nesting places** (this task — where families *are*).
- **Formal worldschooling hubs** (named programs/communities with a site) — note any you
  find as candidates, but keep them separate from organic gathering spots.
- **Curriculum/sightseeing destinations** (Section D) — visited, not nested.

## QUALITY BAR

Cite a source for every claim. Separate what you read from what you inferred. Flag every
season and count as unvalidated. Prefer "I found 3 independent family blogs naming X" over
vague superlatives. When two sources conflict (especially on season), surface the conflict
instead of smoothing it over.

---

## How to use this prompt

- **Seed-agnostic by design.** Specific place names are mostly kept out of Sections A–D so
  a fresh LLM rediscovers them independently (a cross-check on the existing table) rather
  than parroting. The names left in (Israeli sub-type examples, seasonality traps) are
  methodology calibration, not answers to copy.
- **To EXTEND existing work instead of starting clean,** prepend: *"Here is my current
  catalogue [paste `family-gathering-places-2026-06-08.md`]. Validate it, fill the `[lead]`
  sources, and add only NEW places — don't re-derive what's already ranked."*
