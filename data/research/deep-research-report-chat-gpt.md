# Worldschooling and Nesting Family Clusters

## Scope and confidence

This catalogue focuses on **places where traveling, nesting, or worldschooling families actually gather for a season**, not on generic digital-nomad popularity. I treated Hebrew press and Hebrew family-travel/relocation sources as first-class evidence for the Israeli sub-type, and I **de-duplicated obvious repeats at the family/site voice level** when counting, so multiple pages from the same family/site were counted as one voice for a given place unless they added a genuinely separate place or season claim. All **counts and seasons are unvalidated** and should be read as a rough consensus score from the sources I actually read, not as audited population data.

This pass did **not** reach the prompt’s 100+ source aspiration. I truly read and extracted from **34 sources** and catalogued **16 additional leads** for a later pass. The strongest evidence clusters in Southeast Asia and in Hebrew Israeli-relocation media; places like **Koh Phangan, Cascais/Lisbon, and Playa del Carmen** almost certainly merit a deeper second pass, but in this report I only elevate them when the signal was strong enough in what I actually read. The most important methodological result is that **family clustering is often seasonal, place-specific, and mechanism-specific**: some places are organic nesting towns, some are organiser-run pop-ups, and some are hybrid ecosystems where both are true. That distinction matters throughout the ranking below.

## Seasonality and validation traps

The clearest trap in this research is **northern Thailand**. Family evidence does support **Chiang Mai and Pai** as family gathering places, but the same evidence also shows why a naïve “Thailand all winter/spring” summary is wrong. One family source describes Bliss families still being “up north” in Pai in **November**, while another explains that after **three months in Pai** they headed south to **escape the burning season**. Independent Chiang Mai family posts also steer families toward **November to January** and away from **February to April** smoke. In other words, these are real hubs, but not year-round ones, and not even full dry-season ones. citeturn20view2turn21search4turn23view0turn23view1turn22search2

**Hoi An** has a different seasonality problem: it is easy to over-read it as “great all winter” without noticing the flood caveat. The Hoi An family material in this pass strongly supports it as a real family/worldschooling base, but one of the most useful source reads explicitly frames **January to May** as the comfortable run and flags **October–November flooding** as disruptive. That matters because some “best Vietnam months” guides flatten central Vietnam into a generic winter answer, while the family evidence here points to a more specific safe window. citeturn8search0turn9view0turn8search8

**Koh Lanta** and **Pai** show the importance of reading family rhythm rather than tourist rhythm. Koh Lanta’s family clustering is consistently tied to the **northern winter** and extends into late dry season, with one family source giving **November–April** and another saying the return pattern now repeats roughly **December–May**. November appears to function as a transition month when some families are still up north and others are already shifting south. That is exactly the kind of family-season pattern that gets hidden when people quote only tourist weather summaries. citeturn15view0turn20view2turn21search4

**Bansko** must be split into two records rather than averaged. The English worldschooling evidence I read is about a **winter pop-up** built around a three-month cold-season community. The Hebrew Israeli family evidence is different: it describes **summer** Bansko as a place where many Israeli families stop specifically to let their kids have company, with some returning for a period as part of long travel. Averaging those into a single “Bansko family season” would be analytically wrong. citeturn11view1turn13view0

**Bali** is real, but soft around exact timing in this pass. The evidence clearly supports a long-running family/worldschooling ecosystem in and around **Ubud/Sibang**, and it also supports **program-driven** pulses in **Sanur** and at Bali schools that actively welcome traveling families. What I do **not** have from this pass is a strong, independent, place-specific month consensus for the organic family crowd separate from the program calendars. So Bali stays high in the ranking, but its months remain deliberately flagged. citeturn16search0turn16search12turn17view0turn17view1turn18search13turn14search11

**Goa** is strong for the Israeli winter subtype, but this pass did not fully revalidate the precise shoulder months around monsoon reopening. What is solid is that the family/Israeli evidence is **winter-heavy**, that Goa is repeatedly framed as a **soft landing** for families, and that north-Goa Israeli-family infrastructure is substantial enough to support children’s programming and kindergartens during the active season. I would therefore use **Nov–Mar** as the working family-cluster window here, while keeping the exact start/end months flagged for a later pass. citeturn29view0turn29view1turn27view1turn26search6turn29view2
