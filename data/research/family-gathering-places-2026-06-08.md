# Family gathering / nesting places — from personal & family blogs (EN + HE)

A **separate** research output from the hub directory (`candidate-hubs-2026-06-08.csv`).
These are **places where traveling / "nesting" families cluster** — not necessarily
formal worldschooling hubs. The signal is *blog/press consensus*: how many independent
family sources name a place as somewhere families gather, base, or settle for a season.
Researched in **English and Hebrew** (last updated 2026-06-09).

## ⚠️ Families ≠ digital nomads (important)
Digital nomads and traveling/nesting **families are two different communities.** They
overlap only on *working online*; they usually **don't mix**, and their destinations
coincide only sometimes (often just because of fast internet). So:
- The main table counts **family-sourced** references only.
- Pure digital-nomad sources (Nomads.com, nomad-fest listicles) are **excluded** from the
  counts and parked in **Section C** ("nomad hubs, NOT family clustering").
- The `Community` column tags each place: **F** = traveling families · **IF** = notable
  Israeli-family presence · **F+N** = genuine family clustering that also overlaps a nomad
  scene.
- **Nationality skew is its own dimension** (which hub draws which nationality — e.g. Pai
  Israeli, Koh Lanta Swedish): see **Section A-nat**.

## Method & caveats
- **Reference count = # of distinct family sources (key below) I actually read that name
  the place as a place families *gather/nest*** — a rough consensus score, **not**
  popularity/quality, and biased by which posts I fetched.
- **Facebook/WhatsApp groups are not counted** (login-walled) — real clustering at several
  of these (esp. the Israeli ones) is much bigger than the number shows.
- Curriculum/sightseeing-only destinations (Rome, Paris, theme parks…) are in **Section D**.

### Seasonality & validation notes (read before ingesting)
Family-hub season ≠ tourist season. Each place's season above still needs validation; the
biggest traps found so far:
- **Bansko split — now CONFIRMED (was "discrepancy"):** Per the user, Bansko is a *family hub*
  in **summer (Jul–Aug)**; winter is touristic skiing. EN blogs (R8, R12, R14, R36) + Worldschooly
  describe a *winter/spring* program ("gathers fall–winter–spring", sessions Jan 4–Feb 14 &
  Feb 22–Apr 4); R94 (TRVbox, HE) frames Bansko-with-kids around ski season. **A second,
  independent research pass (the ChatGPT report) reproduced the same split from different
  sources**: an English *winter pop-up* source (a 3-month cold-season worldschool community)
  AND a separate Hebrew source ("בנסקו – גרסת הקיץ" / *Bansko – the summer version*) describing
  **Israeli families settling in summer for their kids' company**. So this is settled: **two
  records**, not one — (a) organic Israeli summer community (Jul–Aug), (b) paid winter
  worldschool pop-up (Dec–Mar). Now reflected as the split row #2 above.
- **Northern Thailand burning season** (≈ **Feb–Apr**, peak late-Feb–March, haze can spill
  Dec→June in bad years) empties **Pai & Chiang Mai** — families leave. So their family
  window is the cool, clear **Nov–early Feb**.
- **Monsoon blackouts:** Goa (Jun–Sep), Koh Lanta (May–Oct), SE-Asia broadly; **Hoi An
  floods** Oct–Nov; **Yucatán hurricanes** Jun–Oct.
- **Northern-hemisphere rhythm:** families cluster around **summer (Jun–Aug)** and the
  **Dec–Jan** holidays; European/Israeli summer hubs (Bansko, Romania, Greece, Italy) peak
  then, while tropical hubs peak in the opposite (dry winter) window.
- **Bali season is contested across all three passes** (my dry-May–Sep / GPT Jul–Jan / GEM
  Apr–Oct). Likely cause: organic-family weather window vs. program (Boundless/Bliss) cohort
  calendars vs. nomad rhythm all get blurred together. Needs a place-specific resolution before
  it drives a map filter.
- **⚠️ NEW — Sovereignty / legal-compliance risk (the under-the-radar era is closing).** Gemini's
  most important addition: the parallel Israeli-family institutions are colliding with host-state
  regulation. **Koh Phangan**: the **Arkee/Arki school was raided and shut in May 2026** (an
  unlicensed foreign-run school for Israeli children), amid visa crackdowns and ~10 Israelis
  deported from Thailand. Implication for *us*: a hub can be a real cluster **and** legally
  precarious — that belongs on the record (and arguably as a data flag) so we don't point
  families at something about to be shut. Legal-pathway-clear destinations (**Cyprus, Portugal**)
  contrast with crackdown-exposed SE-Asia enclaves.
- **Program-driven cohorts can fight the local weather** (Boundless runs sequential 3-month
  cohorts year-round): Andalusia/Kotor winter cohorts = cold/wet; Pistoia Jul–Aug = extreme
  heat; La Barra (Uruguay) only works Dec–Mar (S-hemisphere). Don't read a cohort's *availability*
  as a *good family season*.
- **Every source is still UNVALIDATED.** Counts/seasons are blog-derived; confirm before any
  hub is ingested or shown on the map.

## Section A-nat — Which hub skews to which nationality (a distinct dimension)

**Hubs are not nationality-neutral.** Each gathering place tends to draw a *particular* mix,
and that shapes who a family finds there — and crucially **which languages the kids play in**.
A family choosing a base cares whether they'll find their people. Examples that started this:
**Pai = very Israeli** (but not only); **Koh Lanta = very Swedish** (but not only). The core
nationalities seen on the road (user's list): **Israel · USA · Holland · Germany · Sweden ·
England** — plus Russia, Australia, Canada, Scandinavia-broadly recur below.

⚠️ **Caveats — read before trusting this:**
- **Visibility ≠ share.** A community can *feel* dominant (signage, Chabad, a forest school)
  while being a minority of headcount. **Pai** is the clearest trap: it reads as Israeli, but
  UK is the #1 visitor nationality, then US/Germany, with Israelis #2 — they're just highly
  *visible and community-forming* (R105).
- **Tourist nationality ≠ nesting-family nationality.** A place can be 80% Swedish *tourists*
  but a different mix among families who actually base there.
- FB/WhatsApp groups (login-walled) under-count the Israeli/Russian clusters especially.
- All of this is anecdotal/press-derived — **validate before it drives a map filter.**

## Section E — Spanish-immersion family clusters (special cut, 2026-06-09)

A **distinct organizing principle** worth its own section: across Latin America (and parts of
Spain), a whole family-cluster *type* forms **around cheap, structured Spanish schools**. The
mechanism is specific and repeatable — **$5–10/hr one-on-one lessons + homestay with a local
family + afternoon cultural activities** → families and long-stay travelers **stay weeks-to-
months, and the friendships form *through the school itself*** ("people generally stay longer,
so you make really good friends through the Spanish schools" — Sucre). Many schools now run
explicit **family programs** (parents in the adult class, kids with specialist teachers, joint
family sessions). This overlaps existing rows — **Oaxaca (#7), Cusco (#19), Lake Atitlán (#16),
Chinchiná (#23), La Barra (Boundless Spanish-immersion)** — but the *school-as-hub* lens groups
them differently. Best window for the LatAm highlands is **Nov–Apr (dry)**; Costa Rica/Ecuador
run closer to year-round.
